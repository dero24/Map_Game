# Gameplay, streamlined: colour the world, keep what you paint

The plan of record for the game (2026-10-08). Robby's direction, reasoned through with Claude
(2026-10-07), then reviewed and decided with Robby (2026-10-08; the decisions are listed at the
bottom). It is a shorter, tighter version of `GAMEPLAY_VISION.md`: where the two disagree, this one
wins; the vision keeps the detail this leaves out (the rares table, time and weather, the edge
cases, the ideas). Multiplayer details stay in `MULTIPLAYER_PLAN.md` (Robby's `for_mapgame`
folder); section 10 here only says how they fit. The order of work is section 13, and its first
step is the Sea Bright proof of concept (section 14). Add ideas at the bottom.

**In one line:** you travel a real America in a camper van. The world colours in as you look at
it; the few things still in pencil are yours to paint and keep. Your base is the back of the van,
and the paintings you create and hang there are places you can step into.

**The spine:**
- **Colour means seen; pencil means "paint me".** Everything in view is in colour, apart from a
  few things near you still drawn in pencil: kinds you haven't collected yet.
- **One way to collect:** tap a pencil thing when you're close. Animals, objects, trees and plants
  alike.
- **The real world, now, is shared. Your paintings are private.** Step into a painting and you're
  in your own copy of that place, at the moment you created it.
- **Everything you collect has somewhere to go:** your base, your painted places, or the real
  world on your own private layer.

---

## Words we use

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Pencil sketch** | Grey pencil lines on cream paper, with no colour. Places you haven't seen yet look like this, and so do the pencil things |
| **Colouring** | A place turning from pencil sketch into watercolour the first time you see it |
| **Pencil thing** | One of the 3–5 things near you still drawn in pencil: an animal, object, tree or plant of a kind you haven't collected yet |
| **Painting a pencil thing** | Walking up to it and tapping it. It becomes coloured, and its kind is collected |
| **Collecting** | Adding a kind of thing to your collection. A kind is knowledge, not a count: once you have it, you can place as many as you like |
| **Creating a painting** | Pressing Frame and making a picture of what you see. The painting keeps the place, the date, the hour and the weather |
| **Moment** | Something happening that's worth a painting: a heron's strike, gulls lifting off together, the drawbridge up. Each one has a warning sign |
| **Gold edge** | What a painting created during a moment gets |
| **Painted place** | A painting hung on your base's wall. You can step into it |
| **Set** | The kinds of one kind of place: the harbour, the salt marsh, the backyard |
| **Milestone** | Something you've done that grows your base: your first painting in a new region, a completed set |
| **Your layer** | The things you place in the real world. Only you (and friends you invite) see them |

---

## 1. Colouring the world

**Everything colours as you look, from any distance:** the ground, roads, buildings, water, sky,
trees and plants, animals and objects. A place you've never seen is a pencil sketch; the moment
it's in view, it washes into watercolour. It only happens the first time, and it stays coloured
for good. Never a pencil horizon.

**The only pencil left in a coloured place is the few pencil things near you** (section 2), so a
view never looks unfinished.

**People are never pencil and never collected.** They can still appear in your paintings.

**Pencil differs from paint in value and texture, not hue,** so it reads for colour-blind players.
An optional outline setting helps further.

## 2. Collecting: one way, up close

**A few pencil things at a time.** Near you there are 3–5 pencil things (a number to tune): kinds
you haven't collected yet, among the animals, objects, trees and plants around you. Everything
else is in colour, and the far distance always is.

**Picked out of sight.** A new pencil thing is only ever chosen where you're not looking: behind
you, around the corner, further down the beach. Nothing turns to pencil while you watch, so you
discover them as you turn and walk, and you can't stand in one spot tapping. A tree in the
distance is in colour; walk on, look around, and the tree beside the path may be a pencil one.

**A just-collected area goes quiet for a while,** so the next pencil thing tends to be a short
walk away.

**Rares go first.** When something rare is near, it's always one of the pencil things. Among
coloured grey squirrels, a white squirrel still in pencil stands out at once. That's how you know
something rare is near: no icons, no arrows.

**Once you've collected a kind, it's never pencil again,** anywhere.

**The one way to collect:** walk up to a pencil thing and tap it (within about 30 m). It washes
into colour with a brush sound and lifts into your field guide as a card of that moment: the
light, the weather, the season.
- **Too far?** The brush stroke falls short with a little splash. Walk closer; nothing is lost.
- **A moving one** stays pencil while it moves, so it's a gentle chase.
- **On a phone,** a tap picks the nearest pencil thing near where you tapped, so a small bird
  25 m away is easy to hit, and a tap is never mistaken for dragging the view.
- **From the van:** you can paint a pencil thing from the window when you're slow and close.
- **Paintings don't collect.** Frame is for creating paintings (section 4); the tap is for
  collecting. Each tool has one job.

**What can be a pencil thing:** animals; trees and plants; objects outside (benches, lamps, bikes,
boats, cars, signs) and inside buildings (a coat rack, a diner jukebox, a library globe). Every
kind of building has its own things inside, so going in is worth it.

**Rarity is real rarity.** How common an animal is in the game is how common it is there in real
life (the regional abundance tables), in its real range and season. Migrants come and go on their
real dates. Real colour variants (white, black, pale) are the rarest finds.

**Regional things:** the lobster buoy on the coast, the chile ristra in the Southwest. A reason to
travel.

**Sets:** the kinds of one kind of place make a set (the harbour: gull, lobster buoy, dock lantern,
skiff). Sets are data by kind of place, never per town. Completing one grows your base
(section 6).

## 3. Touching the world: everything answers

Tapping a **pencil thing** paints it. Tapping **anything coloured** touches it, and it reacts. The
same gesture means two things, and the picture tells you which.

| Feature | Examples | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Sits** | Benches, docks, stoops, the van's chairs | Sit down; time passes a little faster; shy animals come closer |
| **Rings** | Bells, bell buoys, wind chimes | A sound, and anything nearby notices |
| **Lights** | Porch lamps, lanterns, string lights | Switch on and off; at night, moths come to a light |
| **Feeds** | Gulls, ducks, squirrels, feeders | Toss a crumb: they come, or lift off together |
| **Throws** | Shells, skimming stones, a ball in the park | Throw it; stones skip; a dog may chase the ball |
| **Opens** | Doors, gates, cupboards | Open and close |

- **Animals** react by kind: some flee, some are curious, some ignore you (the shore's fiddler
  crabs already dive into their burrows; turtles already slide off their logs).
- **People** glance at you, wave back, step around you.
- **These features work everywhere:** in the real world, in your base and in your painted places.
  A bench you collected is still a bench you can sit on; a feeder you place still feeds.
- Each family lists its features once, as data, like the foundry's mixes, so it works across the
  lower 48 with no per-town work.

## 4. Creating paintings

- **Press Frame**, compose the view, and create the painting. It keeps the real place, the date,
  the hour, the weather and what was happening in it, as the game's own values so you can step
  into it later, and it goes into your sketchbook. It also colours everything it frames, near and
  far.
- **A gold edge** when a moment is happening in it. Every moment has a **warning sign** a few
  seconds ahead, so timing is a skill you learn by watching, not luck:

| Who | Warning sign | Moment |
|---|---|---|
| Heron | Freezes, neck drawn back | The strike, a fish in its bill |
| Gulls | Bunch up and face the wind | They lift off together |
| Osprey | Hovers over the water | The plunge |
| Deer | Head up, ears forward | The bound away |
| Drawbridge | The bell, the gates coming down | The span rising, for about ten minutes |

- **Moments are data per family,** like the touch features (section 3), so they work across the
  lower 48 with no per-town work. Touching the world is how you cause many of them: toss a crumb
  and the gulls bunch up, then lift.
- **Hang a painting on your base's wall** and it becomes a painted place (section 5). Paintings
  that aren't hung stay in your sketchbook to look at.

## 5. Painted places: step into a painting

**What it is.** Walk up to a painting hanging in your base and step into it. You're standing in
**your own private copy of that real place, at the moment you created the painting**:
- **the real place, kept with the painting:** the map tiles around the spot are saved with it, so
  it never changes when the real world's map is updated, and it opens offline;
- **you can walk about 250 m from where you painted it** (to tune by playing);
- **past that, the world is still there, drawn in pencil,** the way a watercolour stops where the
  wash ends: you see the far shore and the hills as a sketch, but you can't walk out into them;
- **the hour and the weather hold,** so a beach at golden hour stays at golden hour;
- **held, not frozen: the place's life goes on** as it was at that hour. People stroll, cars pass,
  boats come and go, the waves roll, as many as that place has at that hour: a boardwalk painted
  on a summer evening is busy, and the same beach painted at dawn is nearly empty, so when you
  paint decides how lively it is. They're the place's own passers-by, never other players.

**Going in and out.** The painting fills your view as a pencil sketch, then washes into colour
around you, which also covers any loading. To leave, step back through the frame, which hangs
where you came in.

**Public and private.**
- **The real world, now, is shared:** the real date, the real hour, other players (section 10).
- **Your painted places, then, are private,** like the rest of your base. Friends come in only when
  you invite them.

**What you do there.** A painted place is your base's outdoors (the base has no separate outdoor
room):
- **release the animals** you've collected;
- **plant** the trees and plants you've collected;
- **place** objects: a bench by the water, a feeder, lanterns, your boat at the bank.

**Habitats: why your painted places come from different regions.** Every painted place keeps the
kind of place it was painted in: a salt marsh in New Jersey, a desert in Arizona, a maple wood in
Vermont. Every animal belongs to its own kind of place:
- **in a painted place that fits** (a heron in a marsh painting, a lizard in a desert painting),
  with the right plants, water and features (a feeder, a pond), animals are at home: they're
  active, they raise young, and now and then a **rare visitor** comes that you can only find there;
- **in one that doesn't fit,** they're shy and mostly hide. Nothing ever dies or fails.

So travel is how your base grows its habitats: drive into a new region, create a painting there,
hang it, and your base gains that region's kind of place for the animals that belong to it.

**Visitors: new animals that turn up on their own.** Besides the animals you release, a painted
place that fits now and then gets a new animal you didn't bring. Which ones can come depends on
the season you created the painting in, since a painted place stays in that moment forever: a
marsh painted in spring gets spring birds, the same marsh painted in winter gets winter ducks. A
reason to create paintings of the same place in different seasons.

**How many.** Each hung painting needs wall space, and wall space grows with your milestones
(section 6). That's the only limit.

## 6. The base: the back of the van

**The van.** A camper van:
- **the back door** goes straight into the base;
- **the driver's door** goes to the driver's seat;
- **from the driver's seat you can get up and walk into the back.** If a destination is set, the
  van drives itself; if not, it parks.
- **Bigger on the inside:** from outside it's an ordinary camper van; inside, even the first room
  is bigger than the van could hold, and so is every room added later. You find out on the first
  morning: you wake in a room, step out the back door and look back at a small van. The windows
  always show the van's real view at van height, parked or moving.

**It starts small:** a bed, the map table, a little floor and one wall with room for one painting.

**It grows with milestones you can name, not an experience bar:**

| Milestone | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Your first painting in a new region of life (there are 16) | Wall space for one more painted place |
| A completed set | A new room; you choose where it goes |

Travelling is how your wall grows, and collecting is how your rooms grow. A cosy van forever is
fine too: nothing has to be built.

**What's in it:**
- **the bed:** sleep, choose the time you wake (dawn, noon, dusk, night), or sleep until you
  arrive (section 8);
- **the map table:** set a destination, see your coloured map, open your field guide;
- **the painting wall:** your painted places;
- **rooms:** the furniture and objects you collected in real buildings, still working (the lamp
  lights, the chair sits, the chimes ring). Big things like boats and cars go in your painted
  places or the real world, or sit on a shelf as models.

**What you come back to:** young animals and visitors in your painted places, and postcards from
friends.

## 7. Placing things in the real world

- **Your layer:** anything you've collected can be placed in the real world. Only you see it, and
  friends when they're with you. Nothing real is ever removed or changed.
- **It settles where it makes sense:** boats on water, planes where there's room (as they already
  do), cars anywhere, lights and decorations on real structures, plants on soil.
- **It stays until you take it back.** You can always take it back; the kind stays collected.
- **Animals you've collected are released only inside your painted places.** You can place a
  bench or a boat out in the real world, but not your collected animals, so you can't drop
  alligators onto a street in Sea Bright. The real world's wildlife stays where those animals
  really live.

## 8. Travel

**Driving yourself:** real roads, real distances. Everything you see colours.

**The map table:** pick a destination.
- **A place you've already been:** one sleep, and you wake up there.
- **Somewhere new:** the van plans a real route in legs. Each stop is chosen for a reason: a rare
  animal's real range, a building with pencil things inside, a viewpoint, an event. Sleep through
  a leg, or ride awake.
- **Riding awake:** walk around the back while the van drives. It slows and calls out when a
  pencil thing is near the road ("a heron, on the left").
- **Asleep:** you didn't see it, so the route stays uncoloured on your map.

**No fuel gauge** (it's upkeep, not fun) and **no portal gun.** Painted places aren't fast travel:
they take you to your private copy, not the real place today. Getting somewhere new means the
trip.

**The drone (after 1.0).** An FPV drone on the van's roof:
- fly it any time, with a battery of a few minutes that recharges at the van, within a few
  kilometres of it;
- it colours what it sees and can create aerial paintings;
- it collects the same way you do, up close only (section 2);
- **it never marks anything on your map:** finding pencil things and rare animals is always by
  looking;
- its top speed is capped at what tile loading can keep up with.

## 9. The long game

1. **The field guide.** Sixteen regions of life (already data) across the real year: every kind,
   the rare variants, the gold-edged moments and the visitors that only come to painted places. A
   full guide takes a year of real seasons and a trip across the country, and it never runs out
   because the year keeps turning.
2. **Your base.** Rooms furnished from real buildings, and a wall of painted places from every
   region, each one a thriving home for its animals.
3. **The journey (optional story).** The old sketchbook (`GAMEPLAY_VISION.md` §11.3): riddle pages
   of real places that lead you west.

## 10. With other players

`MULTIPLAYER_PLAN.md` and its privacy rules stand as written. How this design fits it:
- **The real world, now, is the shared place:** postcards pinned (delayed, public places only),
  then other players as faint silhouettes. Two players creating a painting of the same moment
  within a minute both get it co-signed.
- **Your base and painted places are private:** invite a friend, and they walk into your van and
  step into your paintings with you.
- **Give a painting:** send a friend a copy; they hang it and can step into it too.
- **A shared map (later):** everyone's coloured map combined into large cells and shown days
  late, so America colours itself in; and an opt-in "first to paint" credit on public landmarks,
  never in a player's starting county.

## 11. The first ten minutes

1. **You wake up in the back of the van:** a bed, the map table, one empty wall.
2. **"Start near you?"** With your consent, the game starts at a public spot in your own town (the
   centre, a park, a viewpoint), found at city level only and never stored. With no consent or no
   fix, you start in Sea Bright.
3. **Step out the back door and look back:** the room you woke in is inside that small camper van.
   The town colours in as you look around. Near the van, a few things are still pencil: a gull on
   a post, a bench, a tree. (These first ones are there as you step out; after them, new pencil
   things are picked out of sight.)
4. **Walk up to the gull and tap it.** It colours and lifts into your field guide.
5. **On the beach, tap the gulls:** you toss a crumb, they gather and bunch up, then lift off
   together. Create a painting as they rise: your first gold edge.
6. **Back in the van, hang it on the wall and step in.** Your own beach, at that moment: the same
   light, people strolling, gulls over the water. Release your gull there.
7. **A note on the map:** "Herons come to the dock at dusk."

## 13. Build order: playable steps, each answering one question

0. **The Sea Bright proof of concept** (section 14; after four fixes to the world that Robby put
   first, and once he has agreed this as the one gameplay plan): the first ten minutes, rough, from
   end to end, behind a switch. *Are ten minutes fun, and does stepping into your own painting make
   people say "whoa"?*
1. **The first minute, built properly:** colouring by sight, the pencil things, collecting, touch
   features for a few families (gull, heron, crab, people, bench, water), moments and gold edges.
   *Are ten minutes fun?*
2. **The van and the first painted place:** the back door to the base, the wall, hanging a
   painting, stepping in (its tiles saved, its hour held and its life going on, pencil past the
   edge), releasing an animal, placing an object. *Do I want to come back tomorrow?*
3. **Habitats and milestones:** fitting and unfitting places, young and visitors, sets, the base
   growing. *Does my base pull me back?*
4. **The road:** the map table, self-driving, sleeping through legs with stops for a reason, a
   second region. *Does travel feel like discovery?*
5. **The field guide and the real calendar** across all sixteen regions, plus placing things in
   the real world.
6. **Multiplayer**, in `MULTIPLAYER_PLAN.md`'s order. The drone after 1.0.

Each step is an item in `feature_list.json` (tier 2), in this order.

**What already exists to build on** (checked in the code, 2026-10-08):
- **colouring:** sketch mode and the explore record (`world/explore.ts`, `render/post.ts`): pencil
  to the horizon, coloured where you walk (a 45 m circle) and wherever a photo frames. It's off by
  default, and colouring everything in view isn't built yet;
- **creating paintings:** photo mode, the sketchbook and its card art (`ui/photo.ts`, `ui/book.ts`,
  `ui/cardArt.ts`). Pages keep the real time and a "when" label, not yet the game's date, hour and
  weather;
- **collecting:** the Almanac (`ui/commissions.ts`) knows kinds of cars, boats, planes, animals,
  trees, flowers and balloons; benches, lamps, signs and things inside have no kind yet. Nothing
  in the world can be tapped yet;
- **animals:** the wildlife sim and the regional abundance tables (`sim/critters.ts`,
  `assets/fauna.ts`): they turn up by real abundance, range and season, and today only ever flee;
- **painted places:** `?at=`, `date=` and `hour=` links and the teleport arrival; the people and
  traffic already run from the hour they're given (`sim/life.ts`), so holding the hour holds their
  mix. Nothing yet holds the clock, sets the weather or keeps the walker inside a radius;
- **inside buildings:** interiors furnished by building type (`world/interior/`), every piece
  static;
- **placing:** the vehicle placing solvers (`player/place.ts`);
- **which kind of place a painting is:** the regions of life and the land cover.

## 14. The Sea Bright proof of concept

**What it is:** section 11, playable and rough, from waking in the van to releasing the gull on
your own painted beach. It's a throwaway prototype behind a switch (`?poc=1`, in Sea Bright), and
the game without the switch is unchanged. Once it has answered its two questions, the parts worth
keeping are built properly in steps 1 and 2.

**Who plays it:** Robby, and two or three people who haven't seen the game.

**Three parts, each playable on its own** (each an item in `feature_list.json`):
1. **The first minute** (`poc-first-minute`): everything in view colours on first sight; 3–5 pencil
   things near you, picked out of sight, among gulls, herons, fiddler crabs, benches and one tree
   kind; tap to paint (the splash when too far); the card into the field guide.
2. **Touch and moments** (`poc-touch-moments`): tap a bench to sit; tap the gulls to toss a crumb:
   they come, bunch up (the warning sign) and lift off together (the moment); a painting created
   then gets a gold edge; paintings keep the game's date, hour and weather.
3. **The van and the painted place** (`poc-painted-place`): the back of the van as one room (a
   bed, the map table, one wall), already bigger than the van, as you see when you step out and
   look back; hang the painting; step in: the hour and weather held, the place's people and
   traffic going on as at that hour, about 250 m to walk, pencil past the edge, the bloom as the
   way in; release the gull; step back out.

**Not in it:** "Start near you?" (it touches privacy, so Robby decides before it's built), the
drone, the real-world layer, habitats and milestones, self-driving, objects inside buildings,
multiplayer, and saving the tiles with the painting (the prototype may load them live).

**What to watch while people play:** how long until the first tap; how often the next pencil thing
turns up; whether people find the pencil things without help; whether they read the gulls' warning
sign; what they say when they look back at the van, and the moment they step into their painting.

## 15. Pacing: the numbers to start from

All to tune by playing; the proof of concept measures the first ones.

| What | Start from |
|---|---|
| Pencil things near you | 3–5, picked 30–150 m away and out of sight |
| A just-collected area stays quiet | about 2 minutes |
| A new pencil thing to find, walking | about one every 2 minutes |
| Collectable kinds in one town | about 60–120: animals, plants, objects, boats and cars, things inside |
| The first ten minutes | 3–5 kinds, one gold edge, one painted place |
| The first hour in a town | about 25 kinds |
| A town's common kinds | about 3 hours; its uncommon ones over a week of visits; its rares across the real year |
| A session | 20–40 minutes, ending on something to come back for (a visitor, a moment at dusk) |

## 16. Open questions (decide by playing)

- 3 pencil things or 5? How far away are they picked? How long does a collected area stay quiet?
- Is 30 m right for tapping?
- How big is a painted place: 250 m from where you painted it, more, or less? Is pencil past its
  edge lovely, or does its far view need colour?
- How many animals in one painted place before a phone slows down?
- How many kinds make a set, and how many sets does a kind of place have?
- Can you create a painting inside a painted place, and hang that too?
- In a painted place, the very same passers-by as in the painting (if the life sim can replay
  them), or the same mix at that hour?
- Do people in a busy painted place make shy animals hide, so a marsh painted at dawn suits them
  better?
