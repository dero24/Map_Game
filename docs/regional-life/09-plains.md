# Great Plains (Kansas, Nebraska, the Dakotas, Oklahoma)

**Covers:** Kansas, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota and Oklahoma (except Oklahoma's Ozark
northeast and Ouachita southeast, which are region 11), plus the plains east of the Rockies in
Montana, Wyoming and Colorado (Billings, Cheyenne, Fort Collins-Greeley east, Pueblo east). Sub-zones:
*tallgrass* (eastern KS Flint Hills, eastern NE, the Red River Valley of ND/MN, central OK); *mixed-grass*
(central: wheat country); *shortgrass* (western KS/NE, eastern CO/WY/MT: ranching); the *Nebraska
Sandhills* (grass-covered dunes and lakes); the *prairie pothole region* (eastern ND/SD: wetlands full
of ducks); the *badlands and buttes* (western SD and ND, Nebraska's Pine Ridge); the *Black Hills*
(SD: a ponderosa-pine mountain island with Rocky Mountain species); and Oklahoma's *Cross Timbers*
(post oak and blackjack oak woodland strips) and Wichita Mountains.

**Feel:** horizon-wide sky and grass; section-line gravel roads; grain elevators and water towers;
farm towns with tree-lined streets as islands in open country; cottonwoods along creeks;
shelterbelts of cedar and elm; wheat, corn, sorghum and sunflowers; wind; summer supercell storms.

**Street and yard trees:** hackberry, honeylocust, green ash (being removed for emerald ash borer in
KS, NE, SD), bur oak, silver maple, red and Freeman maples, Kentucky coffeetree, American and
disease-resistant elms, Siberian elm (INV), cottonwood, catalpa, linden, crabapple, Callery pear (INV,
escaping in OK and KS), redbud (OK state tree), Austrian and ponderosa pine, Colorado blue spruce,
eastern red cedar, lilac hedges (Dakotas), caragana (Siberian peashrub, INV-ish, Dakota windbreaks),
juniper, spirea, mulberry (INV). Lawns: Kentucky bluegrass and tall fescue (north and center), bermuda
(Oklahoma, southern Kansas), buffalograss (western Kansas and Nebraska water-wise lawns).

**Understory shrubs:** wild plum thickets (white in April), chokecherry (ND state fruit), smooth
sumac (scarlet in fall along roads), roughleaf dogwood, coralberry, western snowberry (wolfberry,
the north), buffaloberry (silver leaves, red berries), leadplant, prairie rose, sand sagebrush and
sand plum (sandy west), soapweed yucca (west; spiky blue-green rosettes with tall stalks of white bells
in June), plains prickly pear (low flat pads, yellow flowers June), skunkbush sumac.

**Epiphytes, vines and hangers:** few; poison ivy (thickets along creeks), Virginia creeper and
wild grape in river woods, wild cucumber vines, Boston ivy on old brick downtowns; orange Xanthoria
lichens on fence posts, rocks and grain elevators; no hanging mosses.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter:* tan, buff and copper grass under a huge sky; snow and blowing snow, blizzards in the
  north; bare cottonwoods; dark cedars; green winter-wheat fields in KS/OK; pheasant season.
- *Spring:* Flint Hills burns in March-April (black hills turning emerald green within weeks: a
  signature); pasque flowers; wild plum and redbud (OK/KS); sandhill crane migration; storm season
  begins (April-June: supercells, wall clouds, hail).
- *Summer:* tall grass waving in wind; wheat harvest (June-July) with combines and grain trucks;
  heat; sunflower fields (Aug); cicadas; thunderstorms; fireflies in the east.
- *Fall:* little bluestem copper-red, cottonwoods gold along rivers, smooth sumac scarlet, ash
  yellow-purple; milo rust-red; monarch migration (Sep-Oct); first snow in the Dakotas in October.

### Regional signature

1. Endless grass to the horizon with a lone cottonwood, windmill and stock tank.
2. Flint Hills tallgrass: copper little bluestem in fall, black burns turning emerald in spring.
3. Prairie dog town with a burrowing owl at a mound; badger diggings.
4. Bison herd in Custer State Park or the Tallgrass Prairie Preserve; pronghorn in the west.
5. Western meadowlark singing from a fence post; dickcissels in July.
6. Sandhill cranes by the hundred thousand on the Platte in March.
7. Ring-necked pheasants (INV) in South Dakota ditches; yellow-headed blackbirds and ducks in potholes.
8. Wheat harvest, sunflower fields and grain elevators (with pigeons); shelterbelts of red cedar and Osage orange hedgerows.
9. Scissor-tailed flycatcher and Mississippi kites in Oklahoma towns.
10. Monarchs funnelling south in September; grasshoppers in every step (under supercell skies).

### Range notes

- Prairie dogs, pronghorn, mule deer, black-billed magpies and lark buntings: the western half.
- Scissor-tailed flycatcher: Oklahoma and Kansas (rare in Nebraska).
- Armadillos and feral hogs: Oklahoma, spreading into Kansas.
- Bison and elk only in managed herds; black-footed ferret and swift fox are true rarities.
- Fireflies and periodical cicadas fade out toward the shortgrass west.
- The Black Hills behave like region 12 (ponderosa, mountain goats introduced on Harney Peak/Black Elk Peak, mountain lions, elk).
