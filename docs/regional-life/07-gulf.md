# Gulf Coast and Louisiana bayous

**Covers:** all of Louisiana (Acadiana's prairies and marshes, the Atchafalaya Basin, New
Orleans and the delta, Baton Rouge, the northern pine hills); the Mississippi and Alabama coasts
(Gulfport-Biloxi, Mobile Bay, Gulf Shores) and their southern pine belt; the Florida Panhandle
(Pensacola, Destin, Panama City, Apalachicola, Tallahassee's Red Hills); and the lower Mississippi
alluvial valley (the Mississippi Delta, the Arkansas Delta, West Tennessee and Memphis, Missouri's
Bootheel, NE Louisiana). Southeast Texas (Beaumont, the Big Thicket) shares most of this list but is
in region 8.

**Feel:** cypress-tupelo swamps with Spanish moss and duckweed; live oak alleys and sugar-cane
plantations; bayous with water hyacinth; coastal marsh to the horizon; New Orleans' shotgun houses
with banana trees and crape myrtles; sugar-white quartz-sand beaches and dunes in the Panhandle and
Alabama; flat Delta cotton, rice and soybean fields with catfish ponds and cypress brakes.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter (Dec-Feb):* mild with occasional freezes; cypress bare and gray-brown, live oaks green, Spanish
  moss gray; marsh tawny; Chinese tallow white-seeded; millions of ducks and geese; camellias and
  Japanese magnolia (Feb).
- *Spring (Mar-Apr):* azaleas (Mobile), Louisiana iris, wisteria, crawfish season; cypress needles
  emerge bright lime-green; live oak pollen and leaf drop; spring bird migration fallouts on the coast.
- *Summer (May-Sep):* steamy; duckweed carpets; lotus in bloom; daily storms; crape myrtles; hurricanes
  Aug-Oct (bent trees, blue tarps, piled debris).
- *Fall (Oct-Nov):* subtle: cypress rust-orange, Chinese tallow red, sweetgum and red maple; sugar
  cane harvest with smoky burning fields (Acadiana); cotton white in the Delta (Sep-Oct).

### Regional signature

1. Bald cypress swamp: flared trunks, knees, Spanish moss curtains, duckweed carpet, an alligator's eyes.
2. Live oak alleys with resurrection fern and moss; New Orleans streets with crape myrtles and banana leaves.
3. Brown pelicans on pilings; roseate spoonbills and white ibis in roadside ditches.
4. Crawfish chimneys in yards and crawfish ponds with trap floats; nutria (INV) on canal banks.
5. Water hyacinth (INV) rafts and American lotus on bayous.
6. Black-bellied whistling ducks on wires; prothonotary warblers in the swamp.
7. Chinese tallow (INV) flaming red in fall; sugar cane fields burning.
8. White quartz sand dunes with sea oats on the Panhandle and Alabama coast.
9. Cottonmouths and alligator snapping turtles; green treefrog chorus.
10. Millions of wintering ducks and geese over the coastal marsh.

### Range notes

- Alligators reach up the Mississippi valley to southern Arkansas and SE Oklahoma, but not to Memphis or the Bootheel (strays only).
- Spanish moss reaches north to about southern Arkansas and central Mississippi/Alabama; thin in the northern Delta.
- Nutria: Louisiana and the Gulf coast (also Texas and the Pacific NW); not established in the Panhandle's east.
- Louisiana black bear: mainly the Atchafalaya and Tensas basins.
- Eastern chipmunks and woodchucks: absent from Louisiana and the coast (both occur in West Tennessee and the northern Delta edges); black-capped chickadees absent everywhere here.
