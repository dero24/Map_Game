# Ozarks (and the Ouachitas)

**Covers:** the Ozark Plateau of southern Missouri (Springfield, Branson, Lake of the Ozarks, the
Current and Jacks Fork rivers, the St. Francois Mountains), northern Arkansas (Fayetteville,
Bentonville, Eureka Springs, the Buffalo National River, Mountain View) and northeast Oklahoma
(Tahlequah, the Illinois River, Grand Lake); plus the Ouachita Mountains of west-central Arkansas
(Hot Springs, Mena) and southeast Oklahoma (Talihina, Broken Bow). Southern Illinois's Shawnee Hills
are similar.

**Feel:** rumpled forested hills of oak, hickory and shortleaf pine; deep hollows with clear
spring-fed gravel-bottomed rivers (turquoise pools, sycamores on gravel bars, canoes); limestone and
dolomite bluffs with clinging cedars; caves and huge springs; open rocky "glades" like small deserts
with prickly pear and lizards; small towns, cattle pastures of tall fescue, chicken houses.

**Street and yard trees:** Callery pear (INV; Springfield and NW Arkansas are infamous for escaped
pear thickets turning whole fields white in March), red and sugar maples, pin oak, willow oak,
sweetgum, river birch, flowering dogwood (Missouri state tree) and redbud (spectacular in April along
roads), crape myrtle, southern magnolia (Arkansas), Bradford pear (INV), Leyland cypress, eastern red
cedar, white pine, sycamore, ginkgo, Shumard oak, bald cypress plantings. Shrubs: forsythia, burning
bush (INV), boxwood, azalea, hydrangea, knockout roses. Lawns: tall fescue (Kentucky-31 pastures),
bermuda, zoysia.

**Understory shrubs and small trees:** flowering dogwood, redbud, pawpaw, spicebush, Ozark witch
hazel, deciduous holly (possumhaw), coralberry (buckbrush, purple berries in winter), farkleberry,
low blueberries on ridges, wild hydrangea, aromatic and smooth sumac (scarlet in fall), New Jersey
tea, American hazelnut, Carolina buckthorn, wahoo; invasives: bush honeysuckles (INV), autumn olive (INV),
multiflora rose (INV), Callery pear (INV), sericea lespedeza (INV, in glades and pastures), Chinese privet
(INV, Arkansas).

**Epiphytes, vines and hangers:** poison ivy (big hairy vines), Virginia creeper, wild grape,
greenbrier (catbrier thickets), trumpet creeper, crossvine, Japanese honeysuckle (INV); mosses and
lichens on bluffs and trunks; American mistletoe (C in Arkansas, south Missouri). Kudzu: O in
Arkansas, R in southern Missouri. No Spanish moss (except the southern edge of the Ouachitas, R).

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter:* bare gray-brown oak-hickory hills with green cedars and shortleaf pines; Ozark witch
  hazel blooming on gravel bars; eagles on the lakes; occasional ice storms.
- *Spring:* Callery pear white (INV) in March, then the famous redbud and dogwood show in April
  (redbud magenta on roadsides, dogwood white in the woods); wild sweet William, columbine on bluffs.
- *Summer:* humid green; glades hot with cactus and lizards; fireflies, cicadas, chiggers; floaters
  on clear rivers; gravel bars.
- *Fall:* mid-late October: sugar maple orange in hollows, black gum and sumac scarlet, sassafras
  orange, hickories gold, oaks russet and red; smoketree orange on glades.

### Regional signature

1. Clear spring-fed river with turquoise pools, white chert gravel bars and leaning white sycamores; canoes.
2. Limestone/dolomite bluffs with clinging eastern red cedars, columbines and ferns; cave mouths and springs.
3. Redbud and dogwood in April across oak-hickory hills.
4. Rocky glades with prickly pear, coneflowers, Missouri evening primrose and collared lizards.
5. Elk herd in Boxley Valley.
6. Shortleaf pine ridges (Ouachitas).
7. Armadillos, wild turkeys, eastern bluebirds, whip-poor-wills at night.
8. Copperheads, tarantulas and scorpions as the "wild" edge; chiggers and ticks.
9. Bald eagles wintering on the big reservoirs.
10. Callery pear (INV) white in March.

### Range notes

- Collared lizards, tarantulas, scorpions, roadrunners and painted buntings: southwestern and glade species (not in north Missouri).
- Elk only at Boxley Valley (AR) and the Missouri restoration zone.
- Alligators only at the Ouachitas' southern edge in Oklahoma/Arkansas.
- No Spanish moss, live oak or sabal palm.
