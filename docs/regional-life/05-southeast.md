# Southeast Piedmont and coastal plain (Carolinas, Georgia)

**Covers:** North and South Carolina east of the mountains (Piedmont: Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham,
Greensboro, Greenville-Spartanburg, Columbia; the Sandhills: Fayetteville, Southern Pines,
Aiken; the coastal plain and coast: Wilmington, the Outer Banks, Myrtle Beach, Charleston,
Beaufort, Hilton Head), Georgia south of the mountains (Atlanta, Athens, Macon, Augusta,
Columbus, Savannah, the Golden Isles, south Georgia's wiregrass and the Okefenokee), Virginia's
Southside Piedmont, and the interior hills of Alabama and Mississippi (Birmingham's valleys excepted:
those are region 4), which share most of this list. The Florida border country of south Georgia
already shades into region 6.

**Feel:** loblolly pines over pine-straw beds and orange-red clay; azaleas, dogwoods and crape
myrtles; brick downtowns with willow oaks; blackwater cypress rivers; then the Lowcountry:
live oaks hung with Spanish moss, endless gold-green salt marsh, pluff mud, oyster beds, and
sea islands with palmettos. Long hot humid summers; short mild winters.

**Street and yard trees:** willow oak (Raleigh, "the City of Oaks"; Atlanta, Durham), water and
laurel oaks, live oak (coast and increasingly Piedmont), southern magnolia, crape myrtle (everywhere,
often pollarded), Callery pear (INV), red maple ('October Glory'), Yoshino cherry (Macon GA's
300,000-tree festival; Atlanta), flowering dogwood (NC state flower), redbud, Leyland cypress screens,
loblolly pines towering over yards (pine straw and cones on every roof and lawn), sweetgum, river
birch, ginkgo, saucer magnolia, deodar cedar, sabal palm, pindo palm, windmill palm and Canary
Island date palm (coast streets: Charleston, Savannah, Hilton Head, Myrtle Beach). Shrubs: camellias
(winter flowers red, pink and white), giant Southern Indica azaleas (April spectacle in Augusta,
Charleston, Wilmington), gardenias, boxwood, Indian hawthorn, loropetalum, nandina (INV), red-tip
photinia, wax myrtle, yaupon hedges, tea olive (sweet fall fragrance), Confederate (star) jasmine on
fences, hydrangeas, knockout roses, oleander (coast), lantana; muscadine arbors.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Late winter:* camellias; Callery pear white, then redbud, Carolina jessamine and wisteria (Feb-Mar).
- *Spring:* Yoshino cherries late March; dogwoods and azaleas early-mid April (Masters week in
  Augusta); early April "pollen-pocalypse" when loblolly pollen coats cars, porches and puddles yellow-green.
- *Summer:* long, hot, humid; thunderstorms every afternoon; crape myrtles in bloom; magnolia flowers;
  cicadas and katydids; green marsh; hurricanes possible Aug-Oct.
- *Fall:* late (early-mid November); muted but real: sweetgum purple and red, red maple, dogwood
  burgundy, hickory gold, Chinese tallow scarlet on the coast, bald cypress rust; oaks hold brown; marsh gold.
- *Winter:* mild, short; evergreen pines, magnolias, live oaks, hollies and palmettos keep it green;
  tan bermuda and centipede lawns; bare sweetgums with gumballs; rare snow and ice.

### Regional signature

1. Live oaks hung with Spanish moss and resurrection fern over a Lowcountry street or avenue.
2. Gold-green salt marsh, tidal creek, pluff mud, oyster beds, fiddler crabs and egrets.
3. Loblolly and longleaf pines over pine straw and red clay; brown-headed nuthatches calling.
4. Azaleas and dogwoods in April; crape myrtles in July; Yoshino cherries in Macon.
5. An alligator in a golf-course pond; a green anole flashing its pink dewlap on a porch rail.
6. Cabbage palmettos and sea oats on the dunes; brown pelicans skimming the waves.
7. Kudzu and wisteria (INV) on Piedmont roadsides.
8. Painted bunting at a coastal feeder; white ibis probing a wet lawn.
9. Fire ant mounds (INV) and Joro spider webs (INV).
10. Bald cypress and tupelo in a blackwater swamp with knees.

### Range notes

- Alligators: coastal plain only; the northernmost wild population is around NC's Albemarle Sound (Merchants Millpond, Alligator River). None in the upper Piedmont (Charlotte, Greensboro, Atlanta's north side) except strays.
- Spanish moss and live oak: coastal plain and river towns; thin out inland, essentially absent from the upper Piedmont.
- Venus flytrap: only near Wilmington NC (and a few SC sites); never place elsewhere.
- Red wolf: only NE North Carolina.
- Brown anole (INV) and limpkin (RX) are moving north along the coast.
