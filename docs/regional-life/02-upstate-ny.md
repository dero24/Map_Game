# Upstate New York and the Adirondacks

**Covers:** New York north of the NYC suburbs: the Hudson Valley (Orange/Putnam north to
Albany), the Catskills, the Capital District, the Adirondacks (the Park, 6 million acres), the
North Country and St. Lawrence valley, Tug Hill, the Mohawk Valley, the Finger Lakes, the Southern
Tier, and Western NY (Buffalo, Rochester, the Lake Erie and Lake Ontario plains). NYC and Long
Island are region 3.

**Feel:** most of New England's palette, plus a true boreal north (spruce-fir, black spruce
bogs, tamarack) in the Adirondacks; more dairy farms, vineyards and orchards; deep gorges with
waterfalls and shale ledges in the Finger Lakes; lake-effect snow belts east of the Great Lakes.

**Canopy trees, forest**

**Street and yard trees:** Norway maple (INV), honeylocust, red and Freeman maples, littleleaf
linden, London plane, Callery pear (INV), crabapple, Kwanzan cherry, Japanese tree lilac,
ginkgo, old sugar maples on village streets, Colorado blue spruce, white pine, arborvitae,
weeping willows by ponds. Apple orchards line the Hudson Valley and the Lake Ontario plain;
Concord grape vineyards cover the Lake Erie shore, wine grapes the Finger Lakes slopes.

**Understory shrubs and small trees:** hobblebush, striped maple, witch hazel, shadbush,
alternate-leaf dogwood, red-osier dogwood (red stems on snow), speckled alder, mountain ash,
pin cherry, chokecherry, highbush cranberry, nannyberry, Labrador tea and leatherleaf (bogs),
lowbush and highbush blueberry, mountain laurel (lower Hudson, Catskills, Shawangunks).
Invasives: Japanese barberry, bush honeysuckles, common buckthorn, multiflora rose, burning bush,
swallow-worts (pale and black swallow-wort, INV twining vines that choke central NY fields),
wild parsnip (INV, yellow umbels on roadsides; burns skin), giant hogweed (INV, R, huge white
umbrellas to 4 m, tracked by the state).

**Ground cover:** cool-season lawns; hay-scented, cinnamon, interrupted, sensitive, Christmas,
ostrich and bracken ferns; spring ephemerals (red and white trillium, great white trillium in
western NY woods, trout lily, Dutchman's breeches, bloodroot, hepatica, spring beauty, wild leeks
or ramps carpeting rich woods in April, marsh marigold and skunk cabbage in wet places); north
woods floor of bunchberry, goldthread, wood sorrel, Clintonia, Canada mayflower and sphagnum; bogs
with pitcher plants (O), sundew and cotton grass; fields of goldenrod, asters, milkweed, Queen
Anne's lace and chicory; purple loosestrife (INV) and phragmites (INV) in big marshes (Montezuma);
cattails. Shale ledges with mosses and walking fern in the gorges.

| Plant (regional detail) | Look |
|---|---|
| Wild leeks (ramps) | 20-30 cm pairs of broad smooth green leaves, carpets in April woods before the trees leaf out |
| Skunk cabbage | Purple-mottled hood pushing through snow in March; then huge bright green cabbage leaves |
| Great white trillium | 30-40 cm; three big white petals over three leaves, aging pink; carpets in May |
| Pitcher plant | Red-veined green-maroon tubular leaves in sphagnum, nodding maroon flowers |

**Epiphytes, vines and hangers:** Usnea and other beard lichens on Adirondack spruce and fir;
mosses on glacial boulders; rock tripe on cliffs; poison ivy, Virginia creeper, wild grape,
oriental bittersweet (INV), swallow-wort (INV). Kudzu: a handful of tiny sites in the lower
Hudson Valley (treat as absent).

**Seasonal looks:** fall color earliest in the northeastern US (Adirondack high peaks late
September), Hudson Valley and Finger Lakes mid-late October; tamaracks gold late October. Lake-effect
snow belts (Tug Hill, Buffalo's southtowns, Syracuse, Rochester, Oswego) get 100-300+ inches:
long white winters November-April, snowbanks above cars. Spring arrives late (May in the north);
summer green with hay bales, corn, vineyards; low winter sun on white farms.

**Reptiles and amphibians:** red eft C (Adirondacks/Catskills trails after rain), red-backed
salamander C, spotted salamander O, mudpuppy O (Lake Champlain, St. Lawrence), eastern hellbender R
(Allegheny and Susquehanna drainages of the Southern Tier); spring peeper, wood frog, gray treefrog,
green frog, bullfrog, pickerel and leopard frogs, American toad all C; mink frog O (Adirondack
ponds; smells of onions). Painted turtle C, snapping turtle C, map turtle O (Lake Champlain, St.
Lawrence), wood turtle and Blanding's turtle R. Snakes: common garter C, northern water snake C,
DeKay's brown O, milk snake O, ring-necked O, smooth green O, black rat snake O (Hudson Valley),
timber rattlesnake R (Lake George area, Hudson Highlands, Southern Tier), eastern massasauga R (two
central-NY wetlands), copperhead R (lower Hudson Highlands).

**Insects and invertebrates:** fireflies C (lower elevations, late June-July); black flies C
(Adirondacks May-June) and deer flies; mosquitoes; monarchs C Sum with fall roosts along Lake
Ontario; tiger swallowtails, mourning cloak, fritillaries, white admiral; periodical cicadas by
brood (Brood II Hudson Valley, next 2030); dog-day cicadas and katydids late summer; spongy moth
(INV) outbreaks; emerald ash borer (INV) and hemlock woolly adelgid (INV) widespread; spotted
lanternfly (INV) established in the Hudson Valley and spreading west (Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse
and the Finger Lakes; watched for in vineyards); zebra and quagga mussel shells (INV) crunch
underfoot on Finger Lakes beaches; crayfish in every creek.

**Freshwater "coast":** no seals; Lake Ontario and Lake Erie shores have gulls, cormorants,
Caspian and common terns, shorebirds in late summer; Niagara Falls mist with gulls.

### Regional signature

1. Adirondack lake with loons, spruce-fir and white pine, rocky shore, beaver ponds with drowned gray snags.
2. Sugar maples (state tree) aflame in early October; tamaracks gold in bogs.
3. Black bear at a campground; white-throated sparrow and hermit thrush songs.
4. Finger Lakes gorge: shale ledges, waterfalls, hemlocks and mosses; vineyards on lake slopes.
5. Red efts on wet trails.
6. Lake-effect snow on hemlocks and red farm barns; Holstein dairy cows.
7. Montezuma's snow geese and Canada geese; bald eagles on winter rivers.
8. Wild leeks and trilliums carpeting spring woods.
9. Moose as the rare treat.

### Range notes

- Moose essentially only in the Adirondacks and along the eastern border.
- Hemlock woolly adelgid and spotted lanternfly are spreading, not uniform; treat both as frontier species.
- Boreal birds (Bicknell's thrush, spruce grouse, Canada jay) only in the high Adirondacks.
- No Spanish moss, armadillos, fire ants, anoles; venomous snakes are relict and local.
