# Rocky Mountains (Colorado, Montana, Wyoming, Idaho)

**Covers:** the mountainous parts of Colorado, Montana, Wyoming and Idaho, plus northern New
Mexico's Sangre de Cristo and Jemez mountains and the high Wasatch and Uintas of Utah (which carry
the same species). Includes the Front Range urban corridor (Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins, Colorado
Springs) where plains meet mountains; mountain towns (Aspen, Vail, Steamboat, Estes Park, Jackson,
Bozeman, Missoula, Whitefish, Sun Valley, Coeur d'Alene, Sandpoint); Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Glacier,
Rocky Mountain National Parks. Sub-zones by elevation: *foothills* (grassland, shrubs, ponderosa,
1,600-2,400 m in CO), *montane* (ponderosa, Douglas fir, aspen, lodgepole), *subalpine* (Engelmann
spruce, subalpine fir, lodgepole, 2,700-3,500 m), *alpine tundra* (above treeline, ~3,500 m in CO,
lower further north), *mountain parks and valleys* (sagebrush, hay meadows, willow bottoms), and the
*inland Northwest* of northern Idaho and NW Montana (western redcedar, western hemlock, western
larch: a wetter, PNW-like forest). Eastern plains of these states: region 9. Western Colorado's
plateau, Wyoming's Red Desert basins and Idaho's Snake River Plain: region 14.

**Feel:** dark conifer slopes over sage valleys; golden aspen groves in September; snowy peaks;
rushing rivers with willow and cottonwood; log and stone towns; elk on the golf course; vast open
basins with pronghorn.

### Flora

|---|---|---|

**Street and yard trees:** Front Range and mountain towns: green ash (Boulder and Denver battling
emerald ash borer, INV), honeylocust, Kentucky coffeetree, Norway maple (INV), silver and Freeman
maples, linden, hackberry, bur oak, cottonwood, Siberian elm (INV, alleys), Russian olive (INV),
crabapple, Callery pear (INV), Colorado blue spruce, ponderosa and Austrian pine, aspen clumps in
yards, lilac hedges, chokecherry, Amur maple, serviceberry. Lawns: Kentucky bluegrass (irrigated),
increasingly xeriscape with gravel, native grasses and penstemons; mountain-town yards of native
grass, aspen and spruce.

**Epiphytes, vines and hangers:** beard and hair lichens (wolf lichen, bright chartreuse, on dead
lower branches of fir and pine in Montana and Idaho; dark brown horsehair lichens on spruce); dwarf
mistletoe witches' brooms on lodgepole and ponderosa; few vines: western virgin's bower clematis,
poison ivy (western poison ivy in foothill canyons), Virginia creeper and Boston ivy in towns. No
Spanish moss, kudzu.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter (Nov-Apr):* deep snow in the mountains (ski season), dark green conifers, white bare aspens,
  frozen lakes; Front Range towns with alternating snow and dry brown chinook-wind days; elk and
  bighorns in lower valleys; bald eagles on open rivers.
- *Spring (Apr-Jun):* late; snowmelt rivers roaring brown; pasque flowers and arrowleaf balsamroot on
  foothills; aspen bright lime-green in late May; miller moths in Denver; elk calving.
- *Summer (Jul-Aug):* wildflower meadows (peak July-early August); afternoon thunderstorms daily
  on peaks; green and gold valleys; wildfire smoke some years.
- *Fall (Sep-Oct):* aspens gold, orange and occasionally red in mid-late September (Colorado's main
  event); elk bugling in the rut; western larch gold in October (MT, ID); cottonwoods gold in valleys;
  tundra rust-red; first snows on the peaks.

**Reptiles and amphibians:** few, and absent at high elevations. Western terrestrial garter snake C
(near water, all elevations to ~3,000 m), common (red-sided) garter C, plains garter C (Front Range),
bullsnake/gopher snake C (foothills), prairie rattlesnake O (foothills and lower valleys in CO, WY, MT;
none high up), western rattlesnake O (Idaho canyons), rubber boa O-R (ID, MT, WY; a smooth olive-brown
snake like rubber), racer O. Lizards: short-horned lizard O (greater and pygmy), sagebrush lizard O,
eastern fence/plateau lizard O (Colorado foothills), northern alligator lizard O (ID, MT). Amphibians:
boreal chorus frog C (spring chorus in every pond up to 3,500 m), tiger salamander C (ponds, window
wells), western/boreal toad R (high Colorado, declining), Columbia spotted frog O (MT, ID, WY), northern
leopard frog O, long-toed salamander O (ID, MT), Rocky Mountain tailed frog R (cold streams ID/MT),
bullfrog (INV) in lower reservoirs.

### Regional signature

1. Golden aspen groves in late September against dark spruce and fir; white trunks with black eyes.
2. Ponderosa pine parks with orange jigsaw bark; Abert's squirrels with tufted ears (Colorado).
3. Elk herds bugling in Estes Park and Yellowstone; moose in willow bottoms.
4. Bighorn sheep on roadside cliffs; mountain goats at Glacier.
5. Pikas and marmots on talus; white-tailed ptarmigan on the tundra.
6. Steller's jay, Clark's nutcracker, black-billed magpie, mountain bluebird, broad-tailed hummingbird's trill.
7. Columbines, paintbrush and lupine meadows; fireweed over burns.
8. Beetle-killed gray lodgepole forests; snowpack and rushing rivers with American dippers.
9. Bison, grizzlies and wolves in Greater Yellowstone (the rare tier).
10. Western larch gold and huckleberries in Montana and Idaho.

### Range notes

- Grizzly bears only in the Yellowstone and Glacier ecosystems and small NW Montana/northern Idaho areas; none in Colorado.
- Wolves: Yellowstone, MT, ID, WY common-ish; Colorado only a few dozen.
- Abert's squirrel: ponderosa forests of Colorado, NM and AZ (not MT, WY, ID).
- Moose in Colorado were introduced (1978+) and are now common in the north-central mountains.
- Rattlesnakes only in the lower foothills and canyons; none in the high mountains.
- No fireflies (except tiny isolated colonies), no cardinals, no blue jays beyond the Front Range plains.
