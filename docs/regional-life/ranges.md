# Range limits and the season clock

Rules of thumb for placement, so that nothing appears where it does not live. "Region" numbers
refer to Part B. "Frontier" means the edge is moving (usually north) year by year: treat edge
towns as occasional, not common.

## C6. Known uncertainties (please verify before hard-coding)

- *Moving frontiers:* the exact northern edges of armadillo, Joro spider, spotted lanternfly,
  black vulture, limpkin, great-tailed grackle, brown anole, fire ants and kudzu change every year.
  The edges given here are 2025-2026 public reports; treat border towns as "occasional".
- *Counts that change yearly:* wolves (Colorado ~32 in spring 2026; California 12 packs in March
  2026; Mexican wolves ~319 at the end of 2025), condors, whooping cranes, red wolves, Florida panthers,
  elk herds. Use them as orders of magnitude only.
- *Urban introduced squirrels in the West* (which cities have eastern gray vs fox squirrels) are from
  general knowledge; patterns are right but city-by-city details should be checked on iNaturalist.
- *The black-capped/Carolina chickadee line* and *tufted/black-crested titmouse line* are approximate
  bands, not sharp borders.
- *Periodical cicada brood years* are given only for broods I am confident of; others are omitted.
- *Venomous snakes at range edges* (copperheads in southern New England and Iowa, cottonmouths in
  upland Virginia/North Carolina) are deliberately described as rare; never make them common there.
- *Fireflies in the West:* the Western Firefly Project has found colonies in at least seven western
  states, but they are small, local and not the lawn-filling displays of the East.
