# Midwest and Great Lakes

**Covers:** Ohio (SE Ohio's hills share region 4), Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin,
Minnesota, Iowa, and Missouri north of the Ozarks (St. Louis, Kansas City, Columbia); plus the Lake
Erie shore of Pennsylvania. Sub-zones: the *Corn Belt* (IA, IL, IN, western OH, southern MN, northern
MO: corn and soybeans to the horizon, woodlots and river timber); *cities* (Chicago, Detroit,
Cleveland, Columbus, Cincinnati, Indianapolis, Milwaukee, Minneapolis-St. Paul, Des Moines, St. Louis,
Kansas City); the *Northwoods* (northern MN, WI, Michigan's Upper Peninsula and northern Lower
Peninsula: lakes, pines, birch, bogs); the *Great Lakes shores* (dunes, bluffs, sandstone cliffs,
lighthouses); the *Driftless Area* (SW Wisconsin, NE Iowa, SE Minnesota: steep bluffs and coulees);
and the *Ohio River hills* of southern Indiana and Illinois (oak-hickory, with bald cypress swamps at
the very southern tip).

**Feel:** maple-and-oak shaded neighborhoods with porches; flat fields with farmsteads and red
barns; river towns with bluffs; northern lakes with loons and cabins; four strong seasons with snowy
winters and fireflies in June; huge freshwater "seas" with gulls, dunes and freighters.

**Epiphytes, vines and hangers:** poison ivy (hairy trunk vines), Virginia creeper, wild grape,
Boston ivy on brick (Chicago, Wrigley Field's ivy), English ivy, wintercreeper (INV, smothering trunks
and ground in OH, IN, MO), oriental bittersweet (INV), Japanese honeysuckle (INV, south). Mosses on
north sides of trunks and on sandstone (Pictured Rocks, Driftless); lichens on bark and rocks; Usnea
and old man's beard on Northwoods spruce. Kudzu: rare isolated patches (southern IL, IN, OH; treat as
absent). No Spanish moss.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter (Dec-Mar):* snow cover (deep and long in MN, WI, MI's Upper Peninsula and lake-effect
  belts; patchy in the south); bare gray-brown woods, white corn stubble fields, frozen lakes with ice
  houses; cardinals red on snow; Great Lakes shore ice.
- *Spring (Apr-May):* silver and red maple flowers first; spring ephemerals carpet woods; Callery
  pear white, then redbud, crabapple, lilac; magnolias; green bush honeysuckle (INV) leafs out first;
  morel mushrooms in May; planting season.
- *Summer:* humid, green, tall corn; fireflies (June-July) over every lawn and field; thunderstorms;
  cicadas; lake beaches; mayfly hatches.
- *Fall:* late September (north) to late October (south): sugar maple orange, red maple scarlet,
  oaks russet, hickory and walnut gold; tamaracks gold in northern bogs; soybeans gold, corn tan;
  harvest dust and combines.

**Great Lakes "coast":** no seals or tides; wave-washed beaches, dunes, sandstone and limestone
cliffs, lighthouses and breakwalls; ring-billed and herring gulls, Caspian terns, double-crested
cormorants, shorebirds in late summer; freighters on the horizon; shelf ice in winter.

### Regional signature

1. Maple-and-oak shaded neighborhood with porches; fox squirrels on the lawn; cardinals in snow.
2. Corn and soybean fields to the horizon with red barns, woodlots and fireflies rising at dusk.
3. Bur oak savanna; spring woods carpeted with bluebells, trilliums and mayapples.
4. Northwoods lake: loon, white and red pine, paper birch, aspen gold in fall; black bear and (rare) wolf or moose.
5. Sandhill cranes in Wisconsin and Michigan fields; bald eagles at Mississippi River locks in winter.
6. Great Lakes dunes with beachgrass and buried cottonwoods; ring-billed gulls; zebra mussel shells (INV).
7. Mayfly blizzards in Lake Erie and Mississippi towns.
8. Bush honeysuckle and buckthorn (INV) understory; gray emerald-ash-borer (INV) snags.
9. Monarchs on roadside milkweed; thirteen-lined ground squirrels on golf courses.
10. Warbler fallouts at Magee Marsh in May.

### Range notes

- Wolves, moose, lynx, marten, spruce grouse, Canada jay: the far north only (MN, WI, MI's UP).
- Black-capped chickadee north / Carolina chickadee south of roughly I-70.
- Armadillos, black vultures, Mississippi kites, summer tanagers, copperheads and cottonmouths are southern-edge species.
- Bald cypress only at the extreme southern tip of IL and IN.
- Kudzu and Spanish moss absent.
