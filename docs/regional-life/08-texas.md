# Texas (Hill Country, the coast, the brush country, the Panhandle)

**Covers:** all of Texas, in seven sub-zones that look very different:

| Sub-zone | Towns | Look in one line |
|---|---|---|
| East Texas Piney Woods and Big Thicket | Tyler, Nacogdoches, Lufkin, Beaumont | Region 5/7 pine-hardwood forest with Spanish moss, cypress bayous (Caddo Lake) |
| Gulf Coast and coastal prairie | Houston, Galveston, Corpus Christi, Port Aransas, South Padre | Flat prairie and rice fields, bayous, live oak motts, barrier islands, bays with oyster reefs |
| Blackland Prairie and Cross Timbers | Dallas-Fort Worth, Waco, Austin's east side | Black clay prairie (now farms and suburbs), post oak and blackjack oak woodland strips |
| Hill Country (Edwards Plateau) | Austin west, San Antonio north, Kerrville, Fredericksburg, Wimberley, San Marcos | Limestone hills of live oak and Ashe juniper, clear spring-fed rivers lined with bald cypress, ranch fences, bluebonnets |
| South Texas brush country and Lower Rio Grande Valley | Laredo, Kingsville, McAllen, Harlingen, Brownsville | Dense thorny brush (mesquite, huisache, ebony, prickly pear), subtropical valley with palms, citrus and resacas |
| Rolling Plains, Panhandle and High Plains | Abilene, Wichita Falls, Lubbock, Amarillo | Flat cotton and wheat country, playa lakes, mesquite pastures, prairie dog towns, Palo Duro and Caprock canyons |
| Trans-Pecos | El Paso, Big Bend, Alpine, Marfa, Fort Davis | Chihuahuan Desert with sky-island mountains (Chisos, Davis, Guadalupe); shares much with region 13 |

**Feel:** big skies, live oaks and limestone, mesquite and prickly pear, bluebonnets in spring and
heat in summer; strip-mall parking lots full of great-tailed grackles; ranch gates and windmills;
the most bird species of any state.

**Street and yard trees:** live oak (the default Texas yard and parking-lot tree), cedar elm,
Shumard and Texas red oak, Chinese pistache (red fall color in Dallas and Austin), crape myrtle (every
block), Bradford pear (INV), pecan, bur oak, Monterrey (Mexican white) oak, Arizona ash, cottonwood,
Mexican plum, Texas redbud, desert willow, vitex (lavender spikes), southern magnolia (Houston),
Washingtonia fan palms (Houston, San Antonio, Corpus Christi, the Valley), sabal and Mexican fan
palms, queen palms (Valley), date palms, oleander (Galveston's official flower), citrus in the Valley,
Afghan and Austrian pine windbreaks and Siberian elm (INV) in the Panhandle. Shrubs: Texas sage
(cenizo, silver leaves and purple blooms after rain), yaupon, Texas mountain laurel, nandina (INV),
Indian hawthorn, red yucca (Hesperaloe, coral-red flower spikes), agaves and sotol in xeriscapes,
lantana, turk's cap, salvias, esperanza (yellow bells), knockout roses, Asian jasmine ground cover.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter (Dec-Feb):* gray-green (live oaks and junipers stay green; mesquite and cedar elm bare;
  bermuda tan); Ashe juniper pollen in January; occasional ice storms; sandhill cranes and snow geese.
- *Spring (Feb-May):* huisache yellow (Feb), redbud and Mexican plum, Texas mountain laurel purple
  (March); bluebonnets and paintbrush late March-April; live oaks shed old leaves and flush yellow-green
  with pollen tassels (March); mesquite leafs out last (April); spring storms.
- *Summer (Jun-Sep):* hot (35-40 C), grass golden-brown, cicadas roaring, cenizo blooms after rain,
  crape myrtles, afternoon heat shimmer; hurricanes on the coast Aug-Sep.
- *Fall (Oct-Dec):* late and subtle: Monarch migration (October); cedar elm yellow, flameleaf sumac
  scarlet, Texas red oak and Chinese pistache red, bigtooth maples at Lost Maples (early-mid November),
  cypresses rust (late November-December); Maximilian sunflowers on roadsides.

### Regional signature

1. Live oaks with ball moss over limestone and Ashe juniper; a ranch gate and windmill.
2. Bluebonnets and Indian paintbrush on spring roadsides.
3. Honey mesquite and Texas prickly pear; cenizo blooming purple after rain.
4. Bald cypress on a clear green Hill Country river.
5. Armadillo at dusk; white-tailed deer (and spotted axis deer, INV) in Hill Country towns.
6. Great-tailed grackles roaring in a supermarket parking lot; scissor-tailed flycatcher on a wire; fire ant mounds (INV) in every lawn.
7. Greater roadrunner, crested caracara and golden-fronted woodpecker.
8. Mexican free-tailed bats pouring from Austin's bridge at dusk.
9. The Valley and the brush country: green jays, great kiskadees, plain chachalacas, palms, Texas ebony, javelina.
10. The Panhandle's prairie dog towns and crane-filled playas; the Texas horned lizard as the cherished rare.

### Range notes

- Fire ants, armadillos, scissor-tailed flycatchers, great-tailed grackles: nearly statewide.
- Javelina only south and west (not DFW, Houston or East Texas).
- Green jay, kiskadee, chachalaca, Altamira oriole, red-crowned parrot: the Valley and South Texas only.
- Golden-cheeked warbler only in the Hill Country, March-June.
- Black-crested titmouse in central/south/west; tufted titmouse in the east (they hybridize along the line).
- Alligators: coastal plain and East Texas, inland to roughly Austin/San Antonio/Waco; none in the west.
- Spanish moss east and coast; ball moss central and south.
