# Appalachia (Kentucky, West Virginia, the Smokies, the Blue Ridge)

**Covers:** the Appalachian Plateau, Ridge-and-Valley and Blue Ridge from central and western
Pennsylvania (State College, Altoona, Scranton, Pittsburgh) and western Maryland, through all of
West Virginia, the Shenandoah Valley, Blue Ridge and southwest Virginia, all of Kentucky
(eastern coalfields, the Bluegrass around Lexington, the Pennyrile and Western Coalfield; the far-west
Jackson Purchase shares Gulf lowland species), East and Middle Tennessee (Knoxville, Chattanooga,
Nashville), the western North Carolina mountains (Asheville, Boone), the north Georgia mountains, the
NE Alabama ridges, and southeast Ohio's hills. Elevations from 150 m river valleys to 2,000+ m
spruce-fir summits (Mount Mitchell, Clingmans Dome).

**Feel:** layered blue ridges in haze; rich cove forests of tulip poplar and buckeye; rhododendron
"hells" along mossy boulder streams; hollers with small towns, churches and coal-camp houses;
Kentucky's horse farms with black plank fences and old bur oaks; kudzu-draped valleys in the
south; the world's richest temperate forest for trees, wildflowers and salamanders.

**Street and yard trees:** Callery pear (INV; Kentucky, Tennessee, Ohio Valley roadsides and
interchanges turn white in March), red maple, Norway maple (INV, PA), pin oak, willow oak (TN/KY
cities), southern magnolia (TN, KY), sweetgum, tulip tree, flowering dogwood (Virginia's state tree),
eastern redbud, crape myrtle (TN, KY, VA south), Leyland cypress, white pine screens, Norway and
blue spruce, old white oaks and sugar maples in yards, Kwanzan cherry, Japanese maple, river birch,

**Seasonal looks**

- *Spring:* green climbs the mountains from late March (valleys) to late May (summits); Callery
  pear white in March, then redbud and serviceberry, dogwood mid-April; wildflower carpets; flame
  azalea and mountain laurel May-June; Catawba rhododendron balds mid-June; rosebay rhododendron July.
- *Summer:* hazy blue-green ridges (the "smoke"), humid, lush, thunderstorms; fireflies; ironweed
  and Joe-Pye weed; sourwood flowers; kudzu at full stretch.
- *Fall:* peak early October at the highest elevations to early November in the valleys; sourwood
  and black gum scarlet first, then maples red-orange, hickories and tulip poplar gold, oaks russet
  and brown last. The Blue Ridge Parkway and the Smokies are among the most visited fall sights in the US.
- *Winter:* bare gray-brown ridges stippled with evergreen rhododendron, laurel, pines and hemlock
  ghosts; rime ice on the high peaks; intermittent snow (heavy on the high ridges and the WV plateau);
  brown kudzu tangles; tan pastures.

### Regional signature

1. Layered blue ridges fading into haze from an overlook, with a common raven riding the updraft.
2. Cove forest: tulip poplar, yellow buckeye, big-leaf magnolias, rhododendron along a boulder stream thick with moss (and gray hemlock ghosts).
3. Black bear with cubs; bears walking Gatlinburg and Asheville streets.
4. Elk herds in Cataloochee, eastern Kentucky's mine benches and Benezette.
5. Redbud and dogwood in April; Catawba rhododendron balds in June; sourwood scarlet in fall.
6. Synchronous and blue ghost fireflies; salamanders under every stream rock; box turtles on wet roads.
7. Kudzu-draped southern valleys (INV).
8. Kentucky Bluegrass: Thoroughbreds, black plank fences, limestone walls, old bur oaks and bluegrass pasture.
9. Whip-poor-will at dusk; cerulean warbler high in the oaks.
10. Spruce-fir summits with dead Fraser fir snag fields and dark red spruce.

### Range notes

- Cottonmouths are not in the mountains (only in the far-west lowlands of KY and TN).
- Green anoles only at the southern edge (Chattanooga area and south).
- Spanish moss, live oak, alligators: absent.
- Armadillos spreading into KY and TN; fire ants in southern TN only; Joro spiders spreading north from Georgia.
- Elk are local to restoration areas, not general.
