# Southwest deserts (Arizona, New Mexico, southern Nevada, the Mojave and Sonoran)

**Covers:** southern and central Arizona (Phoenix, Tucson, Yuma, Prescott, Sedona's lower
country), New Mexico except its northern mountains (Albuquerque, Las Cruces, Santa Fe's piñon-juniper
mesas, Roswell), southern Nevada (Las Vegas, the Mojave), California's deserts (Palm Springs and the
Coachella Valley, Joshua Tree, Death Valley, Barstow, the Imperial Valley, Anza-Borrego), Utah's
St. George corner, and (shared with region 8) El Paso and the Trans-Pecos. Four deserts plus their
mountains: the *Sonoran* (saguaro, palo verde; Phoenix, Tucson, Palm Springs), the *Mojave* (Joshua
tree, creosote; Las Vegas, Barstow, Kingman), the *Chihuahuan* (creosote, yucca, agave and grassland;
Las Cruces, El Paso, southern NM), and the *high desert* (piñon-juniper and grassland at 1,500-2,200 m;
Albuquerque, Santa Fe, Prescott). The *sky islands* of SE Arizona and SW New Mexico (Santa Ritas,
Chiricahuas, Huachucas, Catalinas) carry oak, pine and Mexican species. Flagstaff, the Grand Canyon
and the Four Corners plateau are region 14; northern New Mexico's high mountains are region 12.

**Feel:** sun, rock, gravel and spiny green; saguaro-studded hills; stucco and adobe towns with
gravel yards and palms; arroyos (washes) lined with mesquite and palo verde; cottonwood ribbons
along rivers; violent summer monsoon storms and dust; huge night skies; strong pastel winter light.

**Epiphytes, vines and hangers:** desert mistletoe (C: reddish-leafless clumps with pink-red berries
in mesquite, palo verde, ironwood and acacia; phainopeplas feed on it), Arizona grape and canyon
grape in canyons, Virginia creeper (thicket creeper) and western poison ivy in sky-island canyons,
bougainvillea on walls in towns; lichens in bright orange, green and yellow on boulders; no hanging
mosses.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter (Dec-Feb):* mild sunny days, cold nights (frost in the low desert some nights, snow on
  Mount Lemmon, the Four Peaks, and in Albuquerque and Santa Fe); green winter-rye lawns; citrus fruit;
  sandhill cranes in NM; leafless mesquite and cottonwood.
- *Spring (Feb-Apr):* the best season: brittlebush gold, poppies and lupines after wet winters,
  ocotillo red tips, palo verde yellow (April), cactus flowers; citrus blossom smell.
- *Foresummer (May-June):* hottest and driest (40-47 C in Phoenix); saguaro flowers and fruit; brown,
  dusty, dust devils; wildfire season.
- *Monsoon (July-Sep):* towering afternoon thunderstorms, lightning, haboob dust walls, flash floods
  in washes; the desert greens up: ocotillo leaves, spadefoot toads, termite flights at dusk,
  cicadas buzzing.
- *Fall (Oct-Nov):* mild; cottonwoods gold along rivers (November), chamisa yellow in NM, bigtooth
  maples red in sky-island canyons; tarantulas wandering.

### Regional signature

1. Saguaro forest with a Gila woodpecker hole and a cactus wren on top (Sonoran).
2. Joshua trees and creosote flats under a big sky (Mojave).
3. Palo verde blazing yellow in April; brittlebush and poppies on hills after wet winters.
4. Teddy bear cholla glowing backlit at sunset; ocotillo flames.
5. Gambel's quail coveys running across a gravel yard; roadrunner on a wall.
6. Javelina herds in suburban washes; coyotes at dawn on golf courses.
7. Mexican fan palms over stucco and gravel yards; Harris's hawk families on Tucson light poles.
8. Monsoon storm clouds; spadefoot toads chorusing; tarantulas walking.
9. New Mexico: piñon-juniper and chamisa around adobe; cottonwood bosque gold in November with sandhill cranes.
10. Sky islands: coatis, elegant trogons, Arizona sycamores in canyons.

### Range notes

- Saguaro only in the Sonoran Desert (Arizona plus a sliver of California), not in NM, NV or Texas.
- Joshua trees only in the Mojave (CA, NV, NW AZ, SW UT).
- Javelina: AZ south and central, southern NM, Texas; not in the Mojave or Las Vegas.
- Gila monster and desert tortoise are present but rarely seen; treat as regional rares.
- Coatis, elegant trogons, Mexican jays: SE Arizona and SW New Mexico sky islands only.
- Northern cardinals in southern Arizona and NM, absent from Las Vegas and the California deserts (except a few along the lower Colorado River).
- No fireflies to speak of; no gray squirrels (except Arizona gray squirrel in sky-island canyons).
