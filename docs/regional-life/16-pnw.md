# Pacific Northwest (western WA and OR, the Cascades, the dry east side)

**Covers:** Washington and Oregon. *Westside:* the Olympic Peninsula and its temperate rainforests
(Hoh, Quinault, Queets), the outer coast (Long Beach, Astoria, Cannon Beach, Newport, Coos Bay,
Brookings), the Coast Range, the Puget Sound lowlands (Seattle, Tacoma, Olympia, Everett, Bellingham,
the San Juan Islands), the Willamette Valley (Portland, Salem, Corvallis, Eugene) and SW Oregon's
drier Rogue and Umpqua valleys (Medford, Grants Pass: oak, madrone, shading into California). *The
Cascades:* Mount Rainier, Mount Baker, the North Cascades, Mount St. Helens, Mount Hood, the Three
Sisters, Crater Lake, and the Columbia River Gorge cutting through them. *East side:* the Columbia
Basin (Yakima, Wenatchee, the Tri-Cities, Walla Walla), Spokane, the Palouse wheat hills, the
Okanogan, central Oregon (Bend: ponderosa and juniper), the Blue Mountains and Wallowas (NE Oregon).
SE Oregon's high desert (Steens, Malheur) is Great Basin (region 14); northern Idaho's inland rainforest
is region 12; NW California's redwood coast is region 15.

**Feel:** westside: towering dark conifers, everything green and draped in moss, ferns under every
tree, gray drizzle from October to June and gold-brown dry summers, mountains floating over the cities,
salt water, ferries, cedar-shingled houses with moss roofs and rhododendrons. East side: big dry open
country, ponderosa parks, sagebrush, orchards, wheat, basalt cliffs.

**Canopy trees, east side:** ponderosa pine (C; open parklands with orange jigsaw bark from the
Cascade foothills to Spokane and Bend), western larch (gold in October; NE Washington, Blue Mountains,
Okanogan), Douglas fir, grand fir, lodgepole pine, quaking aspen, western juniper (central Oregon:
shaggy, dense, spreading over sage), black cottonwood and willows along rivers, Russian olive (INV),
Siberian elm (INV), black locust and tree of heaven (INV) along roads.

**Street and yard trees:** flowering cherries and plums (Seattle's University of Washington Quad,
Portland's waterfront: pink in March-April), Japanese maple (everywhere), red maple, Norway maple (INV),
London plane, sweetgum, bigleaf maple, red oak, pin oak, Katsura, Persian ironwood, hornbeam, littleleaf
linden, dogwoods (kousa, Pacific, hybrids), purple-leaf plum, Callery pear (INV, escaping in Oregon),
English holly (INV, escaped into every woodland), English and Portugal laurel (INV hedges), deodar
cedar, giant sequoia and coast redwood (planted giants in Puget and Willamette yards), monkey puzzle
trees (Victorian-era yards), Leyland cypress and arborvitae, Douglas fir and western redcedar left as
yard giants. Shrubs: rhododendrons (WA state flower; huge in every yard), azaleas, camellias, pieris,
heathers, hydrangeas, salal, Oregon grape (OR state flower), mock orange, butterfly bush (INV), lavender.
Lawns: perennial ryegrass, fine fescue and bentgrass mixed with moss (moss lawns are normal); green
October-June, brown and dormant July-September unless watered.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Westside winter (Nov-Feb):* gray, mild, drizzly, dark by 4:30 pm; everything saturated green
  (moss, sword ferns, conifers) against bare gray alders and maples; occasional snow and ice storms;
  king tides; bald eagles on salmon rivers; snow geese and trumpeter swans in Skagit fields.
- *Spring (Feb-May):* Indian plum leafs out in February; skunk cabbage, salmonberry flowers,
  red-flowering currant, cherry and plum blossoms (March); Skagit Valley tulip and daffodil fields
  (April); rhododendrons and camas (May); Scotch broom (INV) yellow.
- *Summer (Jul-Sep):* dry and sunny; lawns and grass hills gold-brown; Himalayan blackberries;
  mountain wildflower meadows; wildfire smoke haze some years; low tides and tide-pooling.
- *Fall (Oct-Nov):* rain returns; bigleaf maple butter-yellow, vine maple scarlet, red maple street
  trees, cottonwood gold; western larch gold east of the crest; salmon runs in creeks; mushrooms.
- *East side:* cold, snowy winters; balsamroot springs; hot, dry, golden summers; larch gold in October.

### Regional signature

1. Towering Douglas fir, western redcedar and hemlock with sword ferns underneath.
2. Bigleaf and vine maples dripping with moss and licorice fern in the Hoh rainforest; nurse logs.
3. Salal, Oregon grape and salmonberry; skunk cabbage in March.
4. Banana slugs and rough-skinned newts on the trail; Pacific chorus frogs singing in the rain.
5. Steller's jay, varied thrush, Pacific wren, Douglas squirrel; crows streaming to roost.
6. Bald eagles over salmon rivers; spawning salmon in a city creek.
7. Harbor seals, sea lions, orcas and gray whales; glaucous-winged gulls and pigeon guillemots on piers.
8. Roosevelt elk in an Olympic river valley; Mount Rainier's meadows with hoary marmots; vine maples scarlet in fall.
9. Moss-roofed houses, rhododendrons and flowering cherries; Himalayan blackberry, Scotch broom and English ivy (INV) everywhere.
10. East side: ponderosa parks, western larch gold, sagebrush with balsamroot, black-billed magpies, the Palouse wheat hills.

### Range notes

- Westside lowlands have no rattlesnakes, no flashing fireflies, no cardinals or blue jays, no native gray/fox squirrels in towns (the city ones are INV eastern species).
- Poison oak only in the Gorge, SW Oregon and the Willamette edges; absent in most of western Washington.
- Sea otters on Washington's outer coast only; Olympic marmots only in the Olympics; mountain goats in the Cascades (removed from the Olympics).
- Barred owls (RX) have replaced spotted owls in most forests.
- Moose only in NE Washington; wolves mainly in the northeast corners.
