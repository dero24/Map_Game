# Nationwide building blocks

What the regions share: lawns and the ground under towns, the widespread street and yard trees, shrubs, the wild plants of roadsides and lots, and glossaries of the widespread mammals, birds, reptiles and amphibians, and insects. The region files refer back to these.

## A1. Lawns, turf and the ground under towns

Lawn grass is the single biggest ground-cover surface in any town, and it follows climate:

| Zone | Where | Typical lawn | Look by season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool-season north | New England, NY, northern Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, northern Plains, Rockies towns, Great Basin cities, PNW | Kentucky bluegrass + perennial ryegrass + fine fescue mixes (PNW: ryegrass, fine fescue, bentgrass, lots of moss) | Fine-bladed emerald green spring and fall; dull green to straw in a dry July-August unless watered; green under snow; earliest green in March-April |
| Transition zone | NJ south to VA/NC Piedmont, KY, TN, MO, KS, OK north | Tall fescue (coarse, dark green, clumpy) dominant; some zoysia and bermuda | Tall fescue stays green all winter; zoysia/bermuda yards go tan Nov-Apr, so streets show a patchwork of green and straw in winter |
| Warm-season south | Carolinas, GA, FL, Gulf, TX, OK, southern CA, AZ | Bermuda (fine, wiry), St. Augustine (wide, coarse, blue-green blades, spongy; Florida, Gulf, Texas), centipede (apple-green, low; coastal plain), zoysia, bahia (roadsides, pastures; tall V-shaped seed heads) | Green Apr-Oct; bermuda/zoysia/centipede tan-straw Dec-Mar; St. Augustine in south Florida stays greenish all year |
| Arid west | Desert Southwest, inland California, Great Basin | Gravel or decomposed-granite yards with cactus/agave; irrigated bermuda overseeded with winter ryegrass (green all year in Phoenix/Las Vegas); artificial turf; buffalograss on the Plains edge | Gravel yards are pink-tan to gray; lawns sharply green against desert; summer-brown if water rules bite |

Lawn weeds found almost everywhere: dandelion (INV), white clover, broadleaf and narrowleaf
plantain, crabgrass, violets (east), ground ivy/creeping Charlie (INV, north and east), wild
onion/garlic (south and east), dollarweed (south), sandspurs (south sandy), henbit and purple
deadnettle (purple haze on winter fields in the south and Midwest, Feb-Apr).

Ground colour is also regional and worth painting: orange-red clay in the Southeast Piedmont;
white quartz sand on Gulf and Florida beaches and the NJ Pine Barrens; black soil in the
Corn Belt; red sandstone and pink sand on the Colorado Plateau; caliche and limestone in
Texas; gray-tan desert pavement in the Mojave; dark wet duff and moss in the PNW.

## A2. Widespread street and yard trees

These are planted across many regions; each region notes which dominate there.

| Tree | Where planted | Look (model notes) |
|---|---|---|
| Red maple | Everywhere east of the Plains; PNW, Rockies towns | 12-18 m, oval crown; 3-lobed toothed leaves, red petioles; smooth silver-gray bark; red flower haze in March; brilliant scarlet to orange in fall |
| Silver maple | Midwest, Plains, older Northeast towns | 15-25 m, big spreading, brittle; deeply cut leaves silvery beneath (flash in wind); shaggy gray bark; pale yellow fall |
| Sugar maple | Northeast, Midwest, Appalachia (often old roadside rows) | 18-25 m, dense rounded; 5-lobed leaves with smooth edges; gray furrowed bark; famous orange-red-yellow fall |
| Norway maple (INV) | Northeast, Midwest, PNW, Rockies | 12-15 m, very dense round crown; broad leaves, milky sap; purple 'Crimson King' forms common; holds green leaves late then yellow; seedlings invade woods |
| Freeman / 'Autumn Blaze' maple | Midwest, Plains, Rockies new suburbs | Red x silver maple hybrid; 15 m upright oval; red fall |
| London plane / sycamore | Big cities everywhere (NYC's most common street tree), CA, PNW | 20-30 m; camouflage bark peeling in olive/cream/gray patches; big maple-like leaves; hanging seed balls in pairs; dull brown-yellow fall |
| Honeylocust (thornless) | Everywhere except the deep South coast and desert | 12-18 m, airy vase; tiny pinnate leaflets give dappled light; dark gray plated bark; brief yellow fall, leaves vanish into lawns |

| Japanese zelkova | Northeast and Mid-Atlantic cities | 15-20 m vase; elm-like toothed leaves; peeling bark with orange patches; rusty fall |

| Sweetgum | Mid-Atlantic, South, Texas, CA streets | 18-25 m, pyramidal; glossy star-shaped leaves; corky ridges on twigs; spiky "gumball" seed balls litter sidewalks; purple-red-orange-yellow fall, all on one tree |
| Tulip tree (tulip poplar) | Mid-Atlantic, Appalachia, Midwest south half | 25-35 m (forest giants to 50 m), ramrod-straight trunk; four-lobed square-tipped leaves; green-orange tulip flowers May; clear yellow fall |
| Southern magnolia | Mid-Atlantic south, Gulf, Texas, CA | 15-25 m, dense pyramid; huge glossy evergreen leaves with rusty-brown felt beneath; dinner-plate white flowers May-June; red-seeded cones |
| Crape myrtle | VA/KY south to FL and TX; CA; AZ | 3-8 m multi-trunk; smooth mottled tan-gray-cinnamon bark; cone-shaped flower clusters pink, red, lavender, white Jun-Sep; often pollarded into knuckled stubs ("crape murder"); orange-red fall |
| Live oak (southern) | Coastal VA to FL, Gulf, Texas | See regions 5-8: wide-spreading evergreen with Spanish moss |
| River birch | East and Midwest yards | 10-15 m, multi-trunk; peeling salmon-cinnamon papery bark; diamond leaves; yellow fall |
| White / paper birch (and European white birch) | North, Rockies, PNW yards | 12-15 m; chalk-white bark with black marks; small triangular leaves; yellow fall |

| American elm (and resistant cultivars) | Older town greens, Midwest, Plains | 20-30 m classic vase; arching limbs; doubly toothed lopsided leaves; most old giants killed by Dutch elm disease, survivors are prized |
| Siberian elm (INV) | Plains, Rockies, Great Basin, Southwest high desert | 12-20 m scrappy, brittle; small leaves; seeds pile in drifts in spring; volunteers along alleys and fences |
| Tree of heaven (INV) | Every city east and west; alleys, rail lines, vacant lots | 10-20 m, fast, smooth gray bark; huge pinnate leaves (smell like burnt peanut butter); clusters of tan-pink winged seeds; host of spotted lanternfly |
| White mulberry (INV) | Everywhere | 8-12 m, lumpy; glossy variably lobed leaves; purple-staining fruit on sidewalks in June |
| Black locust | Everywhere (native Appalachia/Ozarks, planted elsewhere) | 12-20 m, open crown; deeply furrowed rope-like bark; small paired thorns; drooping white fragrant flower clusters May |
| Eastern cottonwood / plains cottonwood | Rivers and older towns east of the Rockies; Plains towns | 25-30 m, massive; deeply furrowed gray bark; triangular leaves that rattle in wind; cotton fluff drifts in June; gold fall |
| Weeping willow | Ponds everywhere | 10-15 m, cascading yellow-green curtain to the ground; first green in spring |
| Northern catalpa | Midwest, Plains, older towns | 12-18 m; huge heart-shaped leaves; orchid-like white flower panicles June; long bean pods hanging all winter |

## A5. Widespread mammals glossary

|---|---|---|---|---|
