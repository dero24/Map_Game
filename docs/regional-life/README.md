# Regional life: the reference

What a walker sees growing and living in each region of the lower 48: the flora layer by layer (canopy, street and yard trees, understory, ground cover, hangers and vines, the seasons), the wildlife (mammals, birds, reptiles and amphibians, insects and other invertebrates, what is seen from shore) with how common each is, where and when, and a modelling note, and each region's signature. Researched 2026-10-04 from public sources (state wildlife agencies, USDA PLANTS, the USFS, the NPS, Audubon, iNaturalist); range edges that move every year are flagged in [ranges.md](ranges.md).

The plan that builds on it — what the asset foundry has and lacks, how we measure, the steps — is [docs/REGIONAL_LIFE.md](../REGIONAL_LIFE.md).

## How to read these files

**Status codes** (how often a walker in the right place and season would meet it)

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **C** | Common: seen on most walks in the right habitat and season |
| **O** | Occasional: seen on some walks, needs the right habitat, time of day or luck |
| **R** | Rare / a "regional rare": protected, local, shy or seasonal; worth finding |
| **INV** | Invasive or non-native and naturalised (added to the C/O/R code) |
| **RX** | Range expander: native to North America but spreading into this region recently |

**Bird seasons**: **Res** resident all year; **Sum** summer breeder (about Apr-Sep);
**Win** winter visitor (about Nov-Mar); **Mig** passes through in spring (Apr-May) and/or fall
(Aug-Oct). Two codes mean both (e.g. "Res N, Win S").

**Habitat tags** used in "where": town (downtown, streets), sub (suburbs, yards), park, road
(roadsides, verges, medians), farm (fields, pastures, orchards), wood (woods and forest edge),
wet (pond, marsh, swamp), river, shore (beach, dunes, salt marsh, rocky coast), desert, alpine.

**Heights** are typical mature heights in towns; forest giants noted separately. Colours are
as a painter would see them at walking distance.

**The glossary rule.** Plants and animals that live in many regions (American robin, raccoon,
red maple, dandelion...) are described once, in **Part A**, with their look and behaviour.
Each region then lists them by name with the regional status and anything that differs
there; species found mainly in one or two regions get the full description in that region.
A species not listed in a region should be treated as absent or negligible there. **Part C**
is a range-limit cheat sheet ("never place X north of Y").

### Region boundaries at a glance

named in its region's "Covers" line. Quick map:
