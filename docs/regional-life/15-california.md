# California (coast, Central Valley, Sierra, the south)

**Covers:** California outside its deserts (Palm Springs, Joshua Tree, Death Valley, Imperial Valley:
region 13) and its NE Modoc Plateau (region 14). Sub-zones: the *North Coast and redwoods*
(Humboldt, Mendocino, Del Norte: wet, foggy, PNW-like); the *Klamath-Siskiyou and Shasta* mountains;
the *Bay Area* (San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose, Marin, Napa-Sonoma); the *Central Coast* (Santa
Cruz, Monterey, Big Sur, San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara); the *Central Valley* (Redding, Chico,
Sacramento, Stockton, Modesto, Fresno, Bakersfield); the *Sierra Nevada* and its *foothills* (Gold
Country, Yosemite, Lake Tahoe, Sequoia; the eastern Sierra's Owens Valley shades into region 14);
and *Southern California* (Los Angeles, Orange County, San Diego, the Inland Empire, Ventura, the
Transverse and Peninsular ranges).

**Feel:** a Mediterranean climate: green hills in winter and spring, golden-brown hills from May to
November; oaks scattered over grassland; chaparral-covered mountains; fog banks on the coast; palm-
and jacaranda-lined streets; Spanish-style stucco and ranch houses; almond and walnut orchards and
vineyards; granite and giant conifers in the Sierra; a rocky Pacific coast with kelp, sea lions and
pelicans.

**Street and yard trees (California has the most diverse planted canopy in the US):** Mexican fan
palm (Washingtonia robusta: 25-30 m pencil-thin trunks with small heads; the Los Angeles skyline),
Canary Island date palm (thick trunk, huge feather crown; Hollywood, San Francisco's Dolores Street,
the Embarcadero), California fan palm, queen palm, king palm; jacaranda (LA, San Diego, Orange County:

**Seasonal looks**

- *Winter (Dec-Feb):* the rainy season: hills turn emerald green within weeks of the first big
  rain; tule fog in the Valley; snow on the Sierra and SoCal mountains; mustard and almond bloom
  starts; gray whales migrating south; elephant seals breeding; monarchs clustered in coastal groves.
- *Spring (Mar-May):* peak green; almond orchards a sea of white-pink (late February-early March:
  the Valley's signature); poppies, lupines, ceanothus and wildflowers; jacarandas begin; whales
  heading north with calves.
- *Summer (Jun-Sep):* dry: golden-brown hills dotted with dark green oaks; "June gloom" fog on the
  coast; heat in the Valley (35-42 C) and inland SoCal; jacaranda purple (June), crape myrtles,
  oleanders; buckeyes bare; fire season begins; Sierra wildflower meadows (July).
- *Fall (Oct-Nov):* still brown; Santa Ana winds and wildfire smoke; Sierra aspens and black oaks
  yellow, vineyards red-gold, Chinese pistache and liquidambar color streets; first rains in
  November start the green-up.

### Regional signature

1. Golden summer hills with dark green coast live oaks, valley oaks and blue oaks; green hills in winter.
2. Coast redwoods with sword fern and redwood sorrel in fog; banana slugs.
3. California poppies and lupines in spring; ceanothus blue on chaparral slopes.
4. Chaparral of chamise and red-barked manzanita with California quail and the wrentit's song.
5. Palm-lined Los Angeles streets with jacarandas in purple bloom; bougainvillea on stucco.
6. Eucalyptus groves (INV) with overwintering monarchs.
7. Sea lions barking on piers; sea otters in kelp; harbor and elephant seals; gray whale spouts; brown pelicans and western gulls.
8. California scrub-jay, acorn woodpecker granary tree, Anna's hummingbird, black phoebe, yellow-billed magpie (Valley); lace lichen hanging from coastal oaks.
9. California ground squirrels on every levee and hillside; western fence lizards doing push-ups; almond orchards in bloom.
10. Giant sequoias, granite domes, Steller's jays and black bears in the Sierra.

### Range notes

- Poison oak, not poison ivy, west of the Cascades and Sierra.
- No flashing fireflies; no native gray or fox squirrels in lowland cities (those are INV); no cardinals or blue jays.
- Yellow-billed magpie only in California's Central Valley and coastal valleys (black-billed magpie east of the Sierra).
- Sea otters only on the central coast; elephant seals at a few rookeries; tule elk only at specific reserves.
- Black bears in the Sierra and SoCal foothills, but not the Central Valley floor.
- Sudden oak death and bark-beetle die-off mean visible gray dead trees in many forests.
