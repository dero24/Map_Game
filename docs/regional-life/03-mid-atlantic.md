# Mid-Atlantic coast

**Covers:** New Jersey (all; its northwest Highlands and Kittatinny Ridge share Appalachian
species), New York City and Long Island, Philadelphia and southeast Pennsylvania, Delaware,
Maryland east of the mountains (Baltimore-Washington corridor, the Chesapeake and the Eastern
Shore), Washington DC, Northern Virginia, Tidewater and Hampton Roads, and Virginia's Eastern
Shore. Includes the barrier-island ocean beaches (Long Island's south shore, the Jersey Shore,
Delaware and Maryland beaches, Assateague/Chincoteague, Virginia Beach), the NJ Pine Barrens,
the tidal Chesapeake and Delaware Bays, and the Piedmont cities.

**Feel:** dense old suburbs under big oaks and London planes; tidal salt marsh with osprey
platforms; boardwalk towns with dunes, Japanese black pines and blue hydrangeas; sandy pitch-pine
barrens; Chesapeake watermen country; spring arrives early and summers are humid; winters mild
enough for brant and snow geese, cold enough for occasional snow.

**Street and yard trees:** London plane (NYC and Philadelphia's dominant street tree),
honeylocust, Callery pear (INV, everywhere in NJ suburbs, white in late March), pin oak (the NJ
suburban oak), willow oak (DC, Baltimore, Richmond streets), red maple, Norway maple (INV),
Japanese zelkova, littleleaf linden, ginkgo, American elms (the National Mall's elm rows), Yoshino
cherries (DC Tidal Basin, Branch Brook Park NJ), Kwanzan cherries, crape myrtle (DC, VA, and now
south NJ), southern magnolia (DC southward), flowering dogwood (VA state tree), kousa dogwood,
redbud, Japanese maple, sweetgum, tulip tree, river birch, Leyland cypress and arborvitae
screens, Norway spruce, white pine, holly hedges, tree of heaven (INV) on rail lines and vacant lots.
Shore towns: Japanese black pine (salt-tolerant, picturesque leaning crowns, many dying of pine
wilt), red cedar, bayberry, privet hedges (Hamptons), bigleaf hydrangeas (blue and pink, the
emblem of a Jersey Shore or Long Island summer), rose of Sharon, Rosa rugosa, crape myrtle.

**Seasonal looks**

- *Spring (early):* Callery pear white in late March; lesser celandine and Virginia bluebells;
  Yoshino cherries early April (DC peak late March-early April), magnolias, forsythia; redbud and
  dogwood mid-late April; azaleas and wisteria in May. Tree pollen haze.
- *Summer:* humid and lush; blue hydrangeas, crape myrtles (south), rose mallow; green salt marsh;
  fireflies on lawns; afternoon thunderstorms; beach season.
- *Fall:* late October to mid-November: black gum and sassafras early scarlet, sweetgum purple-red-yellow,
  tulip tree yellow, red maple, pin oak russet; salt marshes turn gold-tan; Pine Barrens blueberry red.
- *Winter:* bare trees with green hollies, pines and red cedars; tan zoysia; brown phragmites;
  ice on the bays in cold years; snow a few times a winter (more in north NJ and Long Island).

### Regional signature

1. Osprey on a nest platform over a gold-green salt marsh; egrets in the marsh pools.
2. Pine Barrens: pitch pine over white sand with blueberry, reindeer lichen and cedar-water streams.
3. Laughing gulls, boardwalk, dunes with beachgrass, poison ivy and Japanese black pines; blue hydrangeas.
4. Horseshoe crabs and red knots on a May beach.
5. Chesapeake blue crabs and diamondback terrapins; brown pelicans in Virginia.
6. London planes and honeylocusts on city streets; pin oaks and Callery pears in the suburbs.
7. Brant on bayside lawns and snow geese in winter.
8. Assateague's wild ponies and sika deer.
9. Spotted lanternfly (INV) in late summer.
10. Fireflies on suburban lawns in June.

### Range notes

- This is the northern edge for many southern species: loblolly pine, bald cypress, live oak and Spanish moss (all only in the far south of the region, mainly Virginia Beach/Eastern Shore), brown pelican (VA common, NJ occasional), boat-tailed grackle, fish crow, Carolina chickadee, cottonmouth (SE Virginia only).
- No alligators, armadillos, fire ants, anoles or kudzu-sized kudzu.
- Coyotes reached Long Island only recently (few); treat as occasional there.
- Black bears: mostly northwest NJ.
