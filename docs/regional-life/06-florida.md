# Florida (peninsula and Keys)

**Covers:** the Florida peninsula from about Jacksonville, Gainesville and Ocala south: north
Florida's pine flatwoods and springs, central Florida (Orlando, Tampa Bay, the Lake Wales Ridge
scrub, citrus and cattle country, the Space Coast), south Florida (Palm Beach, Fort Lauderdale,
Miami, Naples, Fort Myers, the Everglades and Big Cypress), and the Florida Keys. The Panhandle and
Tallahassee are region 7. Climate runs from warm-temperate in the north to tropical in Miami and the
Keys (frost-free), and the plants change with it.

**Feel:** flat, bright and wet; palms and live oaks; retention ponds with alligators and wading
birds in every subdivision; strip malls with royal palms; mangrove shorelines; sawgrass "river of
grass"; turquoise springs; the most non-native animals and plants of any region (escaped pets and
ornamentals everywhere).

**Seasonal looks** (wet and dry seasons matter more than four seasons)

- *Dry season (Nov-May):* blue skies, cooler; sawgrass and prairies brown, wildfires in spring;
  wading birds concentrate at shrinking pools (best birding, Jan-Apr); manatees crowd warm springs and
  power-plant outfalls; winter migrant songbirds everywhere.
- *Spring (Feb-Apr):* live oaks drop leaves and flush yellow-green in March; orange blossom scent
  over citrus; tabebuias yellow and pink; azaleas (north); love bugs in May.
- *Wet season (Jun-Oct):* daily afternoon thunderstorms with towering clouds, lush green, standing
  water, mosquitoes; royal poinciana red in June; hurricanes Aug-Oct (stripped trees, piled debris).
- *Fall/winter colour:* little: red maple, sweetgum and cypress rust in north and central FL
  (November-December); Brazilian pepper red berries; mostly evergreen year-round.

### Regional signature

1. Live oak with Spanish moss and resurrection fern; cabbage palms and saw palmetto under slash pine.
2. Alligator on the bank of a subdivision retention pond, with an anhinga drying its wings nearby.
3. White ibis, wood storks and sandhill cranes walking suburban lawns.
4. Manatees in a turquoise spring; dolphins in the Intracoastal.
5. Mangroves with prop roots; brown pelicans and ospreys; magnificent frigatebirds over the Keys.
6. Green iguanas (INV) on seawalls; brown anoles (INV) on every surface; Cuban treefrogs (INV) on windows.
7. Royal palm boulevards, royal poinciana and tabebuia blooms.
8. Roseate spoonbills; burrowing owls in Cape Coral; Florida scrub-jays in the scrub.
9. Everglades sawgrass with cypress domes and tree islands.
10. Lubber grasshoppers, love bugs, zebra longwings, golden silk spiders.

### Range notes

- American crocodile only in the far south (Florida Bay, Biscayne, Keys); alligators statewide.
- Key deer only in the Lower Keys; Florida panther only in the southwest; scrub-jay only in scrub.
- Green iguanas established only in south Florida's coastal counties; Burmese pythons established south of Lake Okeechobee (Everglades/Big Cypress), DNA evidence of individuals further north.
- No fireflies to speak of in south Florida suburbs; no woodchucks, chipmunks or black-capped chickadees anywhere in the peninsula.
- Spanish moss thins out in the far south; coconut palms and royal palms are south-only (frost kills them north of about Palm Beach/Fort Myers).
